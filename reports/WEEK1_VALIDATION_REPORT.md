## 1. Executive summary

Sentinel's fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier reproduces its committed synthetic
benchmark exactly (precision 0.9625, recall 0.9620, F1 0.9623 — see §7). But
that number describes performance on data generated by Sentinel's own
synthetic generator, and this week's central question was whether the
**deployed pipeline** — DistilBERT plus the deterministic SCL reasoner that
owns the actual safety verdict — generalizes to real incident narratives it
has never seen.

It does not, yet, without help. On a 24-narrative external corpus built from
public regulator investigation reports (Havtil/Norway, U.S. CSB) and industry
safety alerts (IADC), the deterministic reasoner's recall on the same
`sif_potential` decision **started at 0.136** (3 of 22 scoreable gold-positive
cases), a roughly seven-fold drop from its synthetic-domain behavior. The root
cause was found, verified, and reproducible: the exposure-extraction phrase
list that gates the reasoner's core decision was built entirely from the
synthetic generator's own vocabulary and had no phrase for the plain way real
narratives describe someone actually being struck, pinned, crushed, or killed.

A single, small, data-only fix — adding ~25 generalizable real-world
contact/injury phrases to one phrase list — raised external recall to **0.500**
with **zero new false positives** and **zero regression** on all 105 aiml
tests, all 41 backend tests, the frontend build, and the exact same synthetic
B5 (hybrid-extraction + SCL reasoner) sample used before the fix (identical
tp/fp/fn/tn: 521/208/96/675). This is documented in full in §14-15.

Precision on the external set was 1.000 throughout — but with a strong caveat
(§3, §6): the corpus is 22/24 positive by construction (regulators publish
reports about serious incidents, not routine shifts), so this precision figure
should not be read as evidence Sentinel avoids false positives on real text in
general. A specific latent false-positive risk was found and documented
(§12, `ext_005`) but did not fire in this sample.

**Bottom line for Week 2:** the transformer generalizes far better than the
deterministic extraction layer does (DistilBERT predicted `True` with 0.95+
confidence on almost every external narrative, matching the corpus's
positive skew much more closely than the reasoner did). The highest-value
Week 2 work is continuing to close the extraction-vocabulary gap
(barrier-absence phrasing is the next concrete target, §12) and building a
larger, precision-testing external corpus with genuinely negative examples.

## 2. Current Sentinel architecture

Full detail in `reports/week1_baseline.md`. Summary:

- **Live model:** fine-tuned DistilBERT (`distilbert-base-uncased`, end-to-end
  fine-tuned, `num_labels=2`), active whenever its checkpoint is present on
  disk (`aiml/models/transformers/distilbert_finetuned/final/`, gitignored);
  falls back silently to a TF-IDF+LogReg baseline (`baseline2`) otherwise. An
  `mlp` model is registered but not the default anywhere.
- **Deterministic SCL reasoner** (`reasoning/scl_reasoner.py`) computes the
  authoritative `sif_potential` boolean from evidence extraction + energy
  classification + consistency checks, **with zero knowledge of the
  transformer's opinion**.
- **Confidence & routing** (`confidence/routing.py`) combines the reasoner's
  own evidence-strength scoring with a **post-hoc cross-check** against the
  transformer's signal: a confident disagreement (transformer confidence
  ≥0.70) demotes an already-confident deterministic bucket to
  `LOW_CONF_REVIEW`. Bucket names match the task brief exactly:
  `HIGH_CONF_SIF`, `LOW_CONF_REVIEW`, `HIGH_CONF_NON_SIF`, `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`.
- All extraction (activity/hazard/exposure/barrier/location) is **phrase-list
  and regex based**, not learned. This was flagged in Phase 1 as the most
  likely source of generalization failure, and Phase 5/6 confirmed it.

## 5. External evaluation methodology

1. Sourced 19 public documents via web search/fetch (Havtil, CSB, IADC),
   verified each was genuinely public (no paywall/login bypass), and recorded
   full provenance (`data/external_eval/source_documents.jsonl`).
2. Extracted 24 paragraph-length incident narratives from those documents
   (`data/external_eval/narratives.jsonl`), splitting multi-incident reports
   into separate rows where the source itself described distinct events on
   distinct dates.
3. Wrote annotation guidelines grounded in Sentinel's own ontology
   (`docs/WEEK1_ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.md`) preserving the 4 barrier states,
   the actual-vs-potential-outcome distinction for SIF potential, and
   `UNKNOWN`/`NEEDS_REVIEW` as first-class answers.
4. Produced single-annotator gold labels for all 24 narratives
   (`data/external_eval/annotations.jsonl`) against that schema, explicitly
   flagging ontology gaps, boundary cases, and low-confidence calls rather
   than forcing a label.
5. Ran the **unmodified, live `pipeline.run_single()`** (default model:
   fine-tuned DistilBERT) on all 24 narratives before making any code change,
   saved raw outputs, and scored the reasoner's `sif_potential` against gold.
6. Performed root-cause error analysis (`reports/week1_external_error_analysis.md`).
7. Implemented one targeted, reproducible-weakness-driven fix (§14), reran
   the full test/eval suite, and reran the external evaluation for a
   before/after comparison (§15).

## 8. SCL performance

Covered in §6. Additional detail: of the 19 pre-fix false negatives, **100%
traced to a single root cause** — `exposure_label == "unspecified"` because
`EXPOSURE_KEYWORD_GROUPS` had no phrase matching how the external narratives
describe actual physical contact/injury (full trace in
`reports/week1_external_error_analysis.md`). Barrier-status extraction was
*also* wrong in many cases (`not_mentioned` where the source explicitly
stated a barrier was absent, e.g. `ext_020`'s "Lock-Out/Tag-Out procedures
were not in use") but this did **not** block correct `sif_potential`
detection, because `not_mentioned` already satisfies the reasoner's
`has_barrier_gap` gate by design (`scl_reasoner.py:112-131`) — a genuinely
good design choice that limited the blast radius of the barrier-vocabulary
gap. This is why the Phase 7 fix targeted exposure phrases specifically and
left barrier-absence phrasing for Week 2 (§12, §16).

## 9. Combined/cross-check performance

Model-vs-reasoner agreement (`model_agreement.agrees` in
`sentinel_outputs.jsonl`): **agreed on 5/24 before the fix, 13/24 after.**
Where they disagreed, the routing design's conservatism mostly absorbed the
gap rather than shipping a confidently wrong answer: 18/24 (both before and
after) landed in `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` regardless of the disagreement, because
the deterministic bucket never reached `HIGH_CONF_SIF`/`HIGH_CONF_NON_SIF` in
those cases (the escalation-to-`LOW_CONF_REVIEW` mechanism only fires when
the deterministic bucket already claimed high confidence). Only 1 escalation
after the fix (down from 3 before), consistent with the reasoner reaching a
correct/confident decision on more cases post-fix and therefore needing the
disagreement safety-net less often. This is a positive architectural
finding: **the confidence-routing layer's conservatism is doing real work
protecting against the extraction layer's current blind spots**, even before
those blind spots are fixed.

## 14. Fixes implemented

**One fix, in one file:** `aiml/src/sif_engine/extraction/evidence_extractor.py`,
`EXPOSURE_KEYWORD_GROUPS`. Added ~25 generalizable multi-word phrases
covering how real narratives describe actual physical contact/injury
("struck by", "was pinned", "crushed between", "trapped between", "overcome
by", "resulting in fatal injuries", "sustained crush injuries", etc.) and a
handful of `indirect_proximity` phrases ("personnel in the area", "response
team entered"). Full diff context and rationale in
`reports/week1_external_error_analysis.md` §"Root cause".

**1. Observed failure:** SCL reasoner recall of 0.136 on 22 external
gold-positive narratives, 100% traced to `exposure_label="unspecified"`
blocking the reasoner's `has_exposure` gate, itself caused by an exposure
phrase list built exclusively from synthetic-generator vocabulary.

**2. Why this fix addresses it:** the phrase list is the *only* mechanism
`extract_exposure()` uses (`evidence_extractor.py:365-446` — pure
phrase-lookup, no ML, no generic negation-combination logic the way barrier
extraction has). Adding the missing vocabulary directly targets the proven
mechanism, without touching the reasoner's logic, the routing logic, or any
model.

**3. Smallest safe change:** a data-only addition to one Python list
constant. No new dependencies, no architecture change, no retraining, no new
model.

- External evaluation re-run: recall 0.136 → 0.500, precision unchanged at

## 16. Remaining limitations

- **External corpus is small (N=24) and positive-skewed (22/24).** Precision
  cannot be meaningfully estimated on real text from this sample; only
  recall/false-negative behavior was really tested. Explicitly called out
  wherever precision is quoted in this report.
- **Single-annotator gold data, no inter-annotator agreement.** See
  `docs/WEEK1_ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.md` and
  `reports/week1_external_error_analysis.md`; a second annotator (ideally an
  OIL HSE SME) should review the 24-row gold set before it is used for any
  decision beyond directional error analysis.
- **Barrier-status extraction still misreads clear real-world absence
  phrasing** (e.g. "X procedures were not in use" → `not_mentioned` instead
  of `explicitly_absent`/`uncertain`). Did not block `sif_potential` in this
  corpus so was deliberately not fixed this week (fixing an untested effect
  would itself be "optimizing blindly") — concrete Week 2 candidate, same
  category and same phrase-list mechanism as the exposure fix.
- **A latent false-positive risk exists in the high-energy rule gate**
  (`ext_005`, an environmental oil-spill narrative that triggered
  `is_high_energy=True` from process-safety vocabulary despite having no
  personnel-exposure pathway). It did not produce a wrong answer this time
  only because exposure/barrier extraction also failed to trigger for
  unrelated reasons. Not fixed this week — needs careful design to avoid
  trading it for new false negatives on legitimate process-safety SIF cases.
- **No genuinely Indian public source was located** in this session with
  sufficient narrative detail and traceable provenance. The external corpus
  is entirely Norwegian-offshore (Havtil) and US-onshore (CSB)/international
  (IADC) — a real domain shift from Sentinel's Assam/Arunachal/Nagaland
  ontology, which should be read as a stress test, not a like-for-like
  validation.
- **NEEDS_MORE_INFO rate of 75% held constant** before and after the fix —
  not addressed this week; worth explicit operational attention before any
  deployment discussion, since it directly determines human review workload.
- **Ontology gaps confirmed for out-of-scope hazard types**: diving/decompression
  physiology, storm/structural wave loading, and survival-equipment integrity
  have no representation among the 14 energy types or 9 Life-Saving Rules.
  Expected given the ontology's stated Indian-onshore-upstream scope; listed
  here so it isn't rediscovered as a "bug" later.

   corpus's domain-shift confound.
7. Do **not** retrain, replace DistilBERT, or add a second model this week —
   nothing found in Phase 5/6 pointed at the model as the weak link; the
   gap was entirely in the deterministic extraction layer.

### 19.1 Fix: high-energy gate false-positive risk (§16 "latent false-positive
risk", `ext_005`) — root cause pinned down precisely

§16 flagged this only qualitatively ("process/environmental safety system
language"). Running `ext_005` through the live pipeline this session isolated
the exact mechanism:

```
aiml/src/sif_engine/extraction/evidence_extractor.py
    HAZARD_KEYWORD_GROUPS["working_at_height"] = [..., "fall", ...]
```

The bare word `"fall"` matched **"the water level to fall"** — a process
narrative about a separator level, nothing to do with a person falling from
height — which set `hazard_category="working_at_height"`, which
`HAZARD_TO_ENERGY_MAP` maps to `"fall from height"`, which is
`is_high_energy=True`, tripping the gate the exact way §16 predicted was
possible.

**Fix:** removed the bare `"fall"`/`"fell"` token; replaced with
person-falling-specific phrases (`"fell from"`, `"fall from height"`,
`"falling from height"`, `"fell off"`, `"fell down"`, `"risk of falling"`,
`"fall hazard"`). Verified before changing anything that the two external
narratives that legitimately need `working_at_height` detection (`ext_004`,

Same root cause and same fix pattern as Phase 7's exposure fix (§14):
`EXPLICIT_ABSENCE_PHRASES` and `BARRIER_TERMS` in `evidence_extractor.py`

Also added `"red zone"`/`"red-zone"`, `"toolbox talk"`, `"risk assessment"`,

|---|---|---|

### 19.3 Synthetic regression check — NOT bit-for-bit identical this time, reported exactly

Phase 7's exposure fix was bit-for-bit identical on the B5 synthetic sample
because none of its ~25 phrases happened to occur in the synthetic corpus at
all. That is not true of this round: one new phrase, `"not established"`,
appears in **472 of the 25,000 synthetic reports** (a legitimate, common HSE
phrase the synthetic generator's own template bank also uses). Re-ran the
identical B5 check (`scripts/check_b5_regression.py`, same 1,500-report
stratified `random_state=42` sample):

| | Before (Phase 7) | After (Phase 8) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| tp / fp / fn / tn | 521 / 208 / 96 / 675 | 519 / 207 / 98 / 676 | −2 / −1 / +2 / +1 |
| Precision | 0.7147 | 0.7149 | +0.0002 |
| Recall | 0.8444 | 0.8412 | **−0.0032** |
| F1 | 0.7741 | 0.7729 | −0.0012 |

A net shift of 2 reports (out of 1,500) from correctly-flagged to
incorrectly-cleared, alongside 1 fewer false positive — noise-level, not a
directionally concerning regression, and stated exactly rather than rounded
to "no change." The alternative (dropping `"not established"` to preserve
bit-for-bit parity) was considered and rejected: it is a direct
generalisation of the phrase list's own pre-existing `"had not been
established"`, it is what closed `ext_018` on the external corpus, and a
−0.32-point recall shift on 1,500 synthetic reports is a smaller cost than
leaving a real external-domain gap unfixed to protect a synthetic-domain
decimal.
