# External Evaluation Corpus (Week 1)

## What this is

24 narrative excerpts (`narratives.jsonl`) drawn from 19 genuinely public source
documents (`source_documents.jsonl`): regulator investigation reports and industry
safety alerts about real oil-and-gas incidents. This corpus exists to test whether
Sentinel — trained and tuned entirely on synthetic data — generalizes to real
safety narratives it has never seen.

## What this is NOT

- **This is NOT Oil India Limited (OIL) data.** No OIL incident records were used,
  requested, or fabricated. Every source below is a public regulator or industry
  body outside India.
- **This is NOT synthetic data.** It was not generated by
  `aiml/src/sif_engine/data_generation/synthetic_generator.py` or any LLM
  prompted to imitate a report. Every narrative is a factual paraphrase/light edit
  of a specific public document, quoting that document's own language where
  marked, and it is traceable back to that document via `source_doc_id`.
- **This is NOT training data.** It must never be added to `aiml/data/` training
  splits or used to fit model weights. It exists only to evaluate the frozen,
  already-trained pipeline.

## Sources (by organization)

| Organization | Role | Documents | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Havtil (Norwegian Ocean Industry Authority, formerly Petroleum Safety Authority Norway) | Government regulator, Norwegian Continental Shelf | 10 | Investigation reports |
| U.S. Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board (CSB) | Independent U.S. federal investigation agency | 2 | Investigation reports (well blowout, H2S fatality) |
| International Association of Drilling Contractors (IADC) | Global industry association | 7 | Member-submitted safety alerts |

Full provenance (organization, exact title, URL, publication date, incident
date(s), jurisdiction) is recorded per document in `source_documents.jsonl`.
All documents were retrieved via public web search/fetch on 2026-09-16 with no
authentication or paywall bypass.

## How narratives were extracted

For each source document, one paragraph-length narrative was extracted per
distinct incident. Long investigation reports covering multiple wells/incidents,
or a single report describing incidents on two different dates (e.g. the
Oseberg H gangway report, the Goliat diving report, the Wendland well-blowout
report), were split into multiple `narrative_id` rows so each row is one
coherent incident narrative comparable in scope to a single Sentinel report.
Text is a close paraphrase of the source, retaining the source's own causal and
sequential language; it is not embellished, and no detail (barrier status,
outcome, quantities) was invented beyond what the source states.

## Known limitations (documented, not hidden)

- **N=24 is below the 50-200 target** in the Week 1 task brief. Given the
  single-session time available for sourcing and manually verifying
  traceable-provenance public documents, 24 high-quality, fully-traceable
  narratives were prioritized over padding the count with lower-quality or
  loosely-related material. Expanding this corpus (more IADC alerts, PHMSA
  pipeline incident narratives, UK HSE offshore safety notices, additional
  Havtil/CSB reports) is the top candidate for Week 2.
- **Geographic/regulatory skew**: 10 of 19 source documents are Norwegian
  offshore (Havtil); Sentinel's ontology and synthetic data are built around
  Indian onshore/upstream operations (Assam, Arunachal, Nagaland sites). This
  is a genuine domain-shift test, not a like-for-like match — differences in
  outcome should not be over-read as Sentinel-specific defects without checking
  whether they reflect this shift (e.g. unfamiliar equipment names, different
  regulatory vocabulary).
- **No Indian public source was located** in this session with sufficiently
  detailed narrative text and traceable provenance (e.g. PNGRB, OISD, DGMS
  publish statistics and advisories but rarely full narrative investigation
  reports in the public domain). This is a Week 2 follow-up item.
- All source narratives describe **investigation-report-length incidents** or
  **industry safety alerts**, not short-form UA/UC observation cards like
  Sentinel's synthetic data. This is itself a useful stress test (does Sentinel
  degrade on longer, more technical narratives?) but is a distributional
  difference worth tracking separately from ordinary error analysis.

## Files

- `source_documents.jsonl` — one row per source document with full provenance.
- `narratives.jsonl` — one row per extracted incident narrative
  (`narrative_id`, `source_doc_id`, `event_label`, `event_type`, `text`).
- `annotations.jsonl` — gold annotations against the Week 1 schema (see
  `docs/WEEK1_ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.md`). Single-annotator; see that file's
  header for the inter-annotator-agreement caveat.
